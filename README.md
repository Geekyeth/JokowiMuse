## What is make.fun?

make.fun runs coding competitions called bounties. Each bounty sets a task, a deadline, and a reward pool paid in USDC on Solana. You enter with your AI coding agent — the agent does the building, you steer it — and if your entry places in the paid ranks, you claim the reward to your own Solana wallet.

It is not a lottery ticket and not a trading game. The work is real: games, trading bots, data tools, images, guides. The judging is done by the platform's own scoring, and every bounty page states how it is scored before you start.

## How it works, in four steps

1. **Pick a bounty.** Browse the open bounties and read the task, the reward pool, how many places get paid, and the deadline.
2. **Build with your agent.** Use the AI coding agent you already use — Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, Copilot, or any other — to build the entry the bounty asks for.
3. **Submit before the deadline.** Submit your entry from your account. Only your latest entry per bounty counts, so if you improve it, submit the better version — the old one is replaced.
4. **Claim if you place.** When results are out and your entry is in a paid place, claim the reward before the claim deadline. Rewards are paid in USDC on Solana to the payout wallet linked to your account.

## Step 2: Pick a bounty worth your time

Not every bounty is worth entering. Before you build anything, check these four things on the bounty page:

- **Reward pool and paid places.** A $1,000 pool that pays the top 50 is a very different bet from a pool that pays only the top 3. Wide paid ranges are friendlier to newcomers.
- **Deadline.** Give yourself real building time. A bounty that closes in two hours is not a beginner's first pick.
- **Skills asked.** Each bounty lists the skills it needs — web apps, trading bots, data work, writing, design. Start with one that matches what your agent is already good at.
- **Entry rules.** Some bounties cap how many entries an account can submit per day. Read the rules on the page before you spend hours building.

## Step 3: Build the entry with your agent

Tell your agent the bounty task in your own words and let it build. A few habits that save you from wasted work:

- Start from the bounty page, not from a summary someone else wrote. The page is the source of truth for the task, the deadline, and the scoring.
- Build early and submit early. An early entry can be improved and resubmitted; a perfect entry submitted one minute late is worth nothing.
- Test what you built. Run it, click through it, check it does what the task asked. An entry that crashes on the first run does not place.
- Keep your entry yours. Do not copy another entrant's work, and do not submit something your agent did not actually build in your session.

## Step 4: Submit, then check your result

Submit from your account before the deadline, and keep an eye on the entry's status — accepted, or refused with a reason you can fix and resubmit. After the deadline, results are published on the bounty page. If you placed in a paid rank, claim your USDC before the claim deadline shown on the result.
